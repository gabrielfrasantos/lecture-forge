## Title

Clarke Transformation: abc to αβ, and Reading Current-Sensor Errors | PMSM & BLDC — Lesson 12

## Description

```
The space vector from Lesson 11, split into two real signals: α along phase a, β 90° ahead. This lesson derives the Clarke transformation as projections, then shows it geometrically: three phase currents are a point in abc space, balanced currents trace a circle in the plane a + b + c = 0, and looking down the (1,1,1) diagonal turns the three perpendicular axes into the familiar phase axes 120° apart. From there: the full 3×3 matrix with its zero-sequence row and its inverse, amplitude- vs power-invariant scaling (a pure rotation vs a rotation and a shrink, and where the 3/2 in power comes from), and the two-sensor form with i_c = −i_a − i_b and what it silently assumes.

Then sensor errors, computed on screen: a 5 % gain mismatch turns the circle into a tilted ellipse, an offset shifts it (and becomes a ripple at the electrical frequency in the rotating frame), and a 3° timing lag tilts the ellipse the other way. Clarke as it runs on a microcontroller (fixed cost per sample, Q15 constants), and an exercise with a seeded synthetic log (CSV and script in the repository): fit the ellipse with a complex FFT, recover the offsets and the gain ratio, and correct the channels.

Every figure is tagged on screen as calculated, analytic, schematic or historical reconstruction.
```
