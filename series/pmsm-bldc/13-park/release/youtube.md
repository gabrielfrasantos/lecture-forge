## Title

Park Transformation: dq Currents, Rotor Angle and Angle Errors | PMSM & BLDC — Lesson 13

## Description

```
Turn the camera with the rotor, and the rotating current vector from Lessons 11 and 12 stands still: two constant numbers, i_d and i_q. This lesson builds the Park transformation as one complex multiplication, x_dq = x_αβ e^(−jθe), and as a 2×2 rotation matrix with its inverse; puts d on the magnet's north axis and q 90° ahead, and shows why that makes torque depend on i_q alone in a surface-magnet motor (T = 3/2 p λm i_q). Steady state becomes DC, so PI current loops work at any speed; a start-up and a torque step show the difference between the phase and the dq view.

Computed on screen: the 5th and 7th current harmonics both land at 6× the electrical frequency in dq; Lesson 12's current-sensor offset becomes a ripple at the electrical frequency (2.3 % torque ripple at 10 A); where the rotor angle comes from (a 1024-line encoder with 4 pole pairs, three Hall sensors with 60° sectors and ±30° error, interpolation by speed estimate and its lag while accelerating); what an angle error does (10°: 98.5 % torque and a 1.74 A d-current); and the back-EMF in dq, e_q = ωe λm, e_d = 0, which gives a bench check of encoder alignment. Exercise with a seeded synthetic log and Python script in the repository: Park with the encoder angle, with a deliberate 10° error, and with Hall sectors only; a bonus script runs Park on Lesson 12's sensor log, where its offsets appear at the electrical frequency and its gain mismatch at twice it.

Every figure is tagged on screen as calculated, analytic, schematic or historical reconstruction.
```
