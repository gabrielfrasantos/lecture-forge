## Title

Space Vectors: Three Phase Currents as One Rotating Arrow | PMSM & BLDC — Lesson 11

## Description

```
Three phase currents, one complex number. This lesson opens the control half of the series by building the space vector: why per-phase phasors are not enough for a rotating machine, the 2/3 (amplitude-invariant) scaling that makes the vector's length equal the phase amplitude, the power-invariant √(2/3) alternative and which power formula goes with each, the vector as the direction of the stator MMF's fundamental, why the zero sequence is invisible (and why a star connection without neutral carries none), unbalance as an ellipse, and harmonics computed on screen: the 5th turns backward at 5ω, the 7th forward at 7ω.

Then real code on a synthetic, seeded current log (CSV in the repository): a few lines of Python turn two logged phase currents into a space-vector locus, and the complex FFT separates forward from backward rotation. Bench exercise: log two phase currents with current probes and do the same.

Every figure is tagged on screen as calculated, analytic, schematic or historical reconstruction.
```
